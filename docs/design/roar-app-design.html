Roar

Working name for Inside the Game. The crowd is the twelfth player and the loudest camera in the stadium: every phone, pair of glasses and camera in the stands becomes another angle, and AI turns them into one live story you can watch, play and stay safe with.

Name Options
Roar

Short, loud and about the crowd. The mark is a roar as four sound bars, and the AI listens for the roar to find the moment.

Alternatives

  • Twelfth: the crowd as the twelfth player.
  • Terrace: fan culture, the stands as the studio.
  • Angles: says exactly what the product does.

Currency

Crowns. Earned by capturing angles that make a Moment and by playing the live games.

Rules

  • Text on orange, yellow, green and blue is purple, never white (white on orange is only 3.5:1).
  • Pages are white with purple ink. Colour lives in big rounded cards (28px radius), one colour per job.
  • Halftone panels mark where black-and-white crowd and player cut-outs go, as in the collage inspiration.

  • Glasses HUD is the one exception: black background, because black is transparent on the display.

7.2 Where live stats come from
Real stats, in real time

Roar does not produce official stats itself. It pulls them from a licensed football data provider and joins them to the crowd's Moments by match minute.

Providers

ProviderFitNotes
API-Football (api-sports.io)Hackathon and MVPLive fixtures, events, team and player stats over REST. Free tier is rate-limited, so the server polls and fans never call it directly.
football-data.orgPrototype backupSimple and free to start. Check which competitions and how live the free tier is.
SportmonksGrowthLive scores, xG and odds add-ons. Paid plans.
Opta / Stats Perform, SportradarProduction with a league partnerOfficial-grade event data and push feeds. Enterprise contracts.

How it plugs in

  • A new stats-ingest worker (next to media-analysis and moment-engine) polls the provider every 10–15s per live match, normalises it and stores match_stats and match_events in Postgres.
  • Each new event (goal, card, sub, shot) is published to Event Hubs, then to Web PubSub on the match:{matchId} channel. Phones and glasses update without polling.
  • The Moment Matcher uses official events as anchors: a goal event at 72:14 plus fan clips within ±10s becomes the Goal Moment with high confidence.
  • The Storyteller writes Call It questions, quiz questions and the match story from these stats. Every question is checked against data before it goes live, and is settled automatically from the feed.
  • The API key stays in Key Vault. One server-side poll per match serves every fan, which keeps you inside rate limits.

Rules to respect

  • Check the provider's licence covers showing data in a consumer app and running prediction games. Crowns must stay free with no cash value, so it isn't gambling.
  • Show the provider credit wherever stats appear.

8.5 Build guide · glasses, phone and any camera
One web app, every camera

Roar is one responsive web app (installable PWA). The same code serves the phone, the Meta Ray-Ban Display glasses and laptops at the stadium. Only camera access differs per device.

Device matrix

DeviceRuns Roar UI?Camera into RoarInput
Phone (iOS, Android)Yes, browser or installed PWAgetUserMedia and MediaRecorder in the browserTouch
Meta Ray-Ban DisplayYes. Meta's web app path runs plain HTML, CSS and JS on the glasses in a 600 × 600 viewport, opened from a URLThrough the phone companion (below). Web apps on the glasses get motion, phone GPS, Neural Band input and local storage; camera is not listedMeta Neural Band gestures
Ray-Ban Meta, Oakley Meta (no display)No third-party UI on the glassesWearables Device Access Toolkit, a native iOS and Android SDK. Up to 720p at 30fps over BluetoothCapture button, voice through the phone
Laptop, USB or action cameraYes, browsergetUserMedia picks any UVC cameraMouse, keyboard
Network or broadcast cameraNot neededRTSP or SRT into an ingest service that cuts event clips and posts them to POST /mediaNone

Architecture change

  • Move the fan app to the web: Next.js PWA in apps/web for phones, glasses and laptops. Keep apps/admin for the dashboards.
  • Keep apps/mobile (Expo) only as a thin glasses bridge: it wraps the Wearables Device Access Toolkit in a native module, keeps a 15-second rolling buffer from the glasses camera, and uploads a clip through the same API when the fan pinches or AI hears the crowd roar. Same account, same Moments.
  • No-code fallback for camera-only glasses: fans capture with the glasses button, then share the clip from the Meta AI app to Roar. Use the Web Share Target API so Roar appears in the share sheet.

HUD design rules

  • Black means transparent on the waveguide. Use a black ground and bright ink: white, yellow, green, orange. Avoid purple fills, which look dim and muddy.
  • Design for a 600 × 600 viewport and a small 20° field of view. One idea per screen, 3 lines max, body text 30px or larger, headlines in Anton.
  • Every screen should make sense in a 2-second glance. Never autoplay video while the fan is walking (use the motion sensor).
  • Map one gesture per action and always show the hint row: pinch to select, swipe to move, middle pinch to go back, hold pinch for SOS. Confirm exact Neural Band events against Meta's current docs.
  • Keep the bundle small and work offline. Cache the last Moment and queue actions in local storage.

Privacy and safety

  • Short event clips only, never continuous recording (spec section 11). The glasses' capture LED must stay visible.
  • No face recognition or biometric identification (PRD non-goal). Players are identified by shirt number and match data only.
  • SOS from the glasses needs a 2-second hold and sends section-level location only.

Ship it

  • Build and preview the HUD screens in Chrome with Meta's Web App Simulator extension, then open the deployed URL on the glasses.
  • Glasses camera features need the Meta Wearables developer preview. Testing is limited to your organisation until Meta opens publishing more widely.
  • Device support, viewport and gesture details are from Meta's 2026 developer preview. Check developers.meta.com and wearables.developer.meta.com before building.
